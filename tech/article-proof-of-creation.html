Why This Matters: The Centralized Data Graveyard

You don't need to take our word for it. The last decade has been a graveyard of centralized services that held people's irreplaceable data — and lost it.

SoundCloud: In 2020, EDM artist Slushii lost his SoundCloud account to a scammer. Years of releases, comments, + fan interactions — gone. He filed a report with the FBI. No response. The account was officially deleted. His music, his community, his history — erased by a company that didn't prioritize getting it back. (EDMTunes, 2026)

Picosong: A song hosting platform where steel guitar players uploaded their recordings. In November 2019, Picosong went out of business + deleted all their files. Every recording. Every artist. Gone. One forum post summed it up: "I used to post a lot of my recordings onto Picosong. Unfortunately, they went out of business in November and deleted all their files." (Steel Guitar Forum, 2020)

Megaupload: One of the top 15 most popular sites on the internet. Responsible for ~4% of all internet traffic. Then it went out of business, without warning, + a number of people saw their legitimate files disappear into the cyber-ether. Many had not backed up their files. (WeSeeNow)

Dropbox: In 2024, a user lost years of photos of people she loved — some no longer with us. Her email provider went out of business, + Dropbox would only let her recover her password via that email. "I'm absolutely gutted, very upset that I didn't keep the hard copy versions of some of my old photos and trusted they would be safe here." (Trustpilot, 2024)

Samsung Cloud: Gave users until the beginning of July to move their photos, or they were deleted forever. (John The Computer Man)

The pattern is clear: when your data lives on someone else's server, your data is only as safe as their business model, their customer service, + their willingness to fight for you.

Proof of Creation fixes this. Your work is anchored on the BSV blockchain. The blockchain doesn't go out of business. The blockchain doesn't have customer service. The blockchain doesn't delete your files because you forgot to renew your subscription.

What It Does

Proof of Creation anchors your work on the BSV blockchain. That's it. That's the whole thing.

You sign in with your BSV wallet (your private key never leaves your device). You upload a file. You pay a small fee in BSV. Your work is anchored on-chain forever.

Basic: We hash your file (SHA-256 — a 64-character fingerprint) + put that hash on-chain. The file stays with you; the chain proves it existed at time T + is unchanged.

Premium: We put the entire file on-chain. You can rebuild the file from the blockchain alone — no server, no us, just the block. We've recovered a 136 KB photo + an 8 MB GIF straight off the chain.

Anyone in the world can verify your proof with just the transaction ID + the BSV blockchain. No login. No trust in us. The record outlives us.

Why BSV?

Three reasons we chose BSV over other chains:

  1. Unbounded blocks — BSV allows MB-scale data in a single transaction. You can anchor a full MP3, a photo, a video — the entire file, not just a hash.

  2. Cheap data — The on-chain data fee is ~0.1 sat/byte. A 10 MB song is ~20 cents on-chain. The blockchain is essentially subsidizing your storage.

  3. Permanent — The BSV blockchain is decentralized + has been running since 2018. Your proof will outlive your hard drive, your smartphone, + probably your grandchildren's smartphones.
