Why This Matters: The Centralized Data Graveyard

You don't need to take our word for it. The last decade has been a graveyard of centralized services that held people's irreplaceable data — and lost it.

SoundCloud: In 2020, EDM artist Slushii lost his SoundCloud account to a scammer. Years of releases, comments, + fan interactions — gone. He filed a report with the FBI. No response. The account was officially deleted by SoundCloud. His music, his community, his history — erased by a company that didn't prioritize getting it back. (EDMTunes, 2026)

Picosong: A song hosting platform where steel guitar players uploaded their recordings. In November 2019, Picosong went out of business + deleted all their files. Every recording. Every artist. Gone. One forum post summed it up: "I used to post a lot of my recordings onto Picosong. Unfortunately, they went out of business in November and deleted all their files." (Steel Guitar Forum, 2020)

Megaupload: One of the top 15 most popular sites on the internet. Responsible for ~4% of all internet traffic. Then it went out of business, without warning, + a number of people saw their legitimate files disappear into the cyber-ether. Many of these people had not backed up their files. (WeSeeNow)

Dropbox: In 2024, a Dropbox user lost years of photos of people she loved — some no longer with us. Her email provider went out of business, + Dropbox would only let her recover her password via that email. "I'm absolutely gutted, very upset that I didn't keep the hard copy versions of some of my old photos and trusted they would be safe here." (Trustpilot, 2024)

Samsung Cloud: Gave users until the beginning of July to move their photos from the platform, or they were deleted forever. If you wonder where your photos have gone, Samsung is to blame. (John The Computer Man)

The pattern is clear: when your data lives on someone else's server, your data is only as safe as their business model, their customer service, + their willingness to fight for you.

The Tech

Why BSV?

Three reasons:

  1. Unbounded blocks — BSV allows MB-scale data in a single transaction. Bitcoin caps at 1 MB; BSV goes to 2 GB blocks. You can anchor a full MP3, a photo, a video — the entire file, not just a hash.

  2. Cheap data — The on-chain data fee is ~0.1 sat/byte. A 10 MB song is ~20 cents on-chain. A 50 MB video is ~$1. The blockchain is essentially subsidizing your storage.

  3. Permanent — The BSV blockchain is decentralized + has been running since 2018. It's not going away. Your proof will outlive your hard drive, your smartphone, + probably your grandchildren's smartphones.

How It Works

Basic tier: We hash your file (SHA-256 — a 64-character fingerprint) + put that hash on-chain via an OP_RETURN transaction. The file stays with you; the chain proves it existed at time T + is unchanged.

Premium tier: We put the entire file on-chain (BSV's unbounded blocks). You can rebuild the file from the chain alone — no server, no us, just the block. We've recovered a 136 KB photo + an 8 MB GIF straight off the chain.

The Stack

  • Frontend: GitHub Pages (free, fast, CDN) + vanilla JS (no framework, no build step)
  • Backend: Python http.server (stdlib, no framework) + SQLite (no external DB)
  • Blockchain: BSV via WhatsOnChain (free, no-auth API) + bsv-sdk (Python)
  • Tunnel: Cloudflare Tunnel (no Tailscale dependency, HTTPS)
  • Wallet: BSV notary keypair (WIF, 0600 permissions)
  • Auth: BSV wallet login (sign a nonce with your wallet, no password)

The whole thing runs on a single machine (bigboy: AMD 5900X, 128GB RAM, RTX 3090). No cloud. No AWS. No SaaS. Just a machine + the blockchain.
