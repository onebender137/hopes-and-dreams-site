Semax BDNF Heptapeptide

THE MECHANICS

Semax, a heptapeptide analog of ACTH(4-10), hits specific binding sites within the rat basal forebrain. These sites exhibit high affinity with a dissociation constant (KD) of 2.4 nm and a BMAX value around 33.5 fmol/mg protein. Crucially, calcium ions are required for this interaction, highlighting the role of calcium-dependent mechanisms in Semax's action.

After intranasal application at doses between 50 to 250 micrograms per kilogram of body weight, Semax rapidly boosts BDNF levels within three hours, especially in the basal forebrain. This effect is absent in the cerebellum, underscoring regional specificity. The upregulation of BDNF suggests enhanced neuroplasticity and synaptic transmission, bolstering cognitive functions.

THE BIOLOGICAL LEVERAGE

Semax's impact extends beyond just increasing BDNF; it also modulates trkB expression in the hippocampus. A single application at 50 microg/kg body weight significantly alters BDNF and trkB levels, indicating its role in neural plasticity and synaptic transmission. By stimulating neuronal growth and survival via trkB receptor activation, Semax enhances cognitive capabilities through neurotrophic factor pathways.

Enhanced BDNF expression supports synaptic plasticity, a cornerstone of learning and memory processes. This upregulation, coupled with the presence of unique binding sites for calcium ions, positions Semax as a key player in cognitive enhancement and potential neurological disorder treatment.
