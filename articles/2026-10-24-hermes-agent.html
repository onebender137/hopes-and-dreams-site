Meet Hermes: The Open-Source AI Agent That Actually Runs on Your Machine

The Problem with "Cloud AI"

Almost every AI assistant you've ever used lives in someone else's cloud. You type a message, it "thinks," and you get an answer back. But the thinking happens on their servers, with their models, under their rules. Your words flow through their infrastructure. Your "personal" assistant is really a shared service you're renting.

That's fine for casual use. But if you care about privacy, self-reliance, or building something that's genuinely yours, it's a hard ceiling. You can't audit the model. You can't change the rules. You can't run it when their servers are down. And the moment they decide your use case is "not allowed," you're locked out of your own tool.

Enter Hermes Agent

Hermes Agent is an open-source personal AI agent built by Nous Research. The core difference is simple: it runs on your hardware. Your machine, your models, your rules. You talk to it from wherever you want — your phone, your watch, your terminal, your Discord, your own custom channels.

And it's not a chatbot. It's a full agent. It can:

  • Chat and reason across a long context window
  • Run tools — execute code, read and write files, browse the web
  • Manage your calendar, email, and tasks
  • Control smart-home devices and physical hardware
  • Remember you across sessions (persistent memory)
  • Learn reusable procedures it saves as "skills"
  • Be extended with plugins that add entirely new capabilities

On top of all that, it's model-agnostic. You can point it at any model — a local one running on your own GPU, or a cloud API. The agent is the constant; the brain is swappable.

How to Install

The whole install is one line:

curl -fsSL https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.sh | bash

Then a one-time setup, and you're running:

hermes setup   # configure your model + channels
hermes         # start the agent

The installer handles the environment, the dependencies, and the config. There's no cloud account to create, no telemetry to opt out of.

Requirements

  • Python 3.10+
  • A model backend — local (llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM) or cloud (any OpenAI-compatible API)
  • ~2 GB disk for the agent itself
  • Optional: a GPU if you want to run local models at speed

That's the whole list. No proprietary runtime, no vendor lock-in.

Why Hermes

  • Open source — you can read every line, audit it, and fork it.
  • Self-hosted — your hardware, your data, your rules.
  • Model-agnostic — swap the brain without swapping the agent.

  • Multi-platform — Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack, Signal, iMessage, CLI, and fully custom channels.
  • Extensible — plugins, skills, and persistent memory let it grow with you.

Our Angle: The Danny Chat App

We didn't just install Hermes — we built our own integration around it. The danny-push app is a native Android app that talks to our agent over a private tailnet. No cloud, no middleman, no third-party server touching the conversation. Just our phone talking to our agent, end to end.

We also flashed a small hardware gadget that connects straight into the agent — a physical device you can hold that's wired into the same brain. Ours is a Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-1.28: a board the size of a matchbook with a color display, a touch layer, and a motion sensor, running a custom firmware that bridges it into the agent. But the Hermes Gadget SDK is designed to be board-agnostic — breadboard builds, CYD boards, Waveshare LCDs, and a whole family of ESP32 hardware can all be wired into the same brain. You can browse the supported boards and the flashing guide at the Hermes Gadget SDK site. That's the "sovereign tech" part made concrete: not a service you rent, but a stack you built yourself, from the phone app down to the silicon.

The Bigger Picture

This is what "sovereign AI" looks like in practice. Not a subscription you log into, but a system you own. Your hardware, your models, your data, your rules — and an agent that remembers you, learns your workflows, and extends the moment you want it to.

Hermes is the foundation. The plugins, skills, and custom integrations are the building blocks. And the result is an AI assistant that's actually yours.

In the next few posts we'll pull the hood off some of the deeper work — reverse-engineering tools wired into the agent, per-command sandboxing, and the custom channels we built from scratch. This is just the intro.

Builder's Note

Start local, go cloud when you need to. Point Hermes at a local model (llama.cpp or Ollama) for full privacy, then swap in a cloud API for heavy lifting. The agent stays the same — only the brain changes. That's the whole point of model-agnostic.
