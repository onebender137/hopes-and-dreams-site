Circadian Entrainment: Low-Dose Melatonin Protocols

The human circadian rhythm is a 24-hour biological cycle regulated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus. Melatonin, a hormone produced by the pineal gland, serves as the primary signal for "biological night." However, standard high-dose supplementation often results in receptor desensitization and morning lethargy.

The Phase-Shift Protocol

Melatonin is not a sedative in the traditional sense; it is a chronobiotic. To successfully shift the circadian phase (e.g., to combat jet lag or shift work), timing is more critical than dosage. Administering melatonin in the late afternoon or early evening can advance the clock, while early morning administration can delay it.

The Efficacy of Low-Dose (0.3mg)

Research indicates that the brain's melatonin receptors (MT1 and MT2) are most responsive to physiological doses, typically around 0.3mg to 0.5mg. Standard pharmacy doses of 5mg-10mg are pharmacological megadoses that can disrupt natural hormone production and lead to vivid, often disruptive, dream cycles without improving sleep quality.

Light: The Master Switch

Melatonin production is acutely suppressed by blue light (450-480nm). For effective circadian entrainment, supplementation must be paired with blue light blocking protocols 2 hours before the target sleep time. This ensures that the supplemental melatonin works in harmony with the body's natural rising levels.
