Glycine + NAC Methionine Restriction Mimetic

THE MECHANICS

Excessive muscle meat consumption propels methionine levels upwards, driving oxidative stress and aging. Methionine's abundance in muscle tissue contrasts with its scarcity in glycine-rich collagenous tissues such as connective tissue, bone marrow, and skin. Glycine deficiency exacerbates this issue by limiting a crucial glutathione precursor, thereby intensifying oxidative damage. Supplementing with glycine not only mitigates methionine-induced oxidative stress but also sustains redox balance through enhanced glutathione synthesis.

Methionine metabolism converts it into homocysteine, which can either be remethylated to return as methionine or oxidized to form cysteine. Glycine serves as a precursor for glutamate, contributing to glutathione (GSH) biosynthesis via cysteine pathways. NAC, an acetylated cysteine derivative, directly boosts cysteine availability, further supporting GSH synthesis when paired with glycine.

THE BIOLOGICAL LEVERAGE

Glycine supplementation can be optimized in doses ranging from 3 grams to 50 grams daily, with methionine restriction mimicking achieved through a lower range of 2-4 grams per day. This strategic combination leverages the body's redox balance by increasing glutathione production.

Dietary adjustments complement this protocol by reducing methionine intake through balanced consumption of collagen-rich tissues and plant-based proteins, which are inherently lower in methionine but maintain essential amino acid profiles. These practices support sustained metabolic efficiency while mitigating oxidative stress associated with high-methionine diets.

Start with modest doses, gradually increasing based on individual tolerance and response. Incorporate a diet rich in collagen-rich tissues such as bone broth and organ meats alongside plant-based proteins like legumes and nuts. This dual approach maximizes redox balance support while minimizing oxidative stress.
